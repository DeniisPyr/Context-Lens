1. PURPOSE
==========

This SOP describes the routine maintenance, calibration and troubleshooting of the Vestorra LX-500 HPLC system.
Its aim is to keep the system in a qualified state, so that analytical results produced on it are reliable.
It also describes what to do when the system shows an error code or fails a calibration test.


2. SCOPE
========

This SOP applies to all Vestorra LX-500 HPLC systems in the Quality Control Laboratory at the Brno site.
It covers the binary pump, degasser, autosampler, column compartment and UV/VIS detector of the system.
It does not cover the chromatography data system, which is maintained under SOP-IT-0031.
It does not cover the qualification of HPLC columns, which is described in SOP-QC-0118.
Repairs inside the modules are outside the scope of this SOP and are done only by the service provider.


3. RESPONSIBILITIES
===================

3.1 Laboratory Operations Manager

- Makes sure that enough trained staff and spare parts are available for the maintenance in this SOP.
- Approves the annual maintenance and calibration plan for all LX-500 systems.
- Decides on the purchase of replacement systems and major spare parts.

- Owns this SOP and keeps it up to date.

- Perform the daily and weekly maintenance tasks before and after using the system.
- Record every maintenance task, error code and repair in the instrument logbook.
- Stop using the system and inform the QC Instrument Specialist when an error cannot be cleared by this SOP.

3.4 Quality Assurance (QA)

- Reviews and approves this SOP and its revisions.
- Reviews impact assessments after a failed calibration or a major repair.
- Approves the return to service of a system after an out-of-tolerance calibration result.

3.5 Service provider

- Performs the annual preventive maintenance and all repairs inside the modules.
- Provides a signed service report after every visit.
- Uses only spare parts approved by the manufacturer.

5. SAFETY
=========

5.1 Wear a lab coat, safety glasses and nitrile gloves for all maintenance tasks in this SOP.
5.2 Handle organic solvents in a ventilated area and keep them away from sources of ignition.
5.3 Before opening any fluid connection, stop the flow and let the system pressure fall below 1 MPa.
5.4 Never look directly into the light of the deuterium lamp; it emits ultraviolet radiation.
5.5 Let the deuterium lamp cool for at least 15 minutes before removing it, because the lamp housing is hot.
5.6 Collect solvent waste in the labelled waste container and dispose of it according to SOP-EHS-0007.
5.7 Switch off and unplug a module before removing any cover; only the service provider may open covers
    marked with a high-voltage warning.
5.8 Clean up spills immediately with absorbent pads and report larger spills to the EHS department.


6. SYSTEM DESCRIPTION AND SPECIFICATIONS
========================================

6.1 Modules

The LX-500 system consists of the following modules, stacked in this order from the bench up:

- LX-P2 binary pump with two pump heads (pump A and pump B) and an integrated purge valve
- LX-DG four-channel vacuum degasser
- LX-AS autosampler with a cooled sample tray, needle wash port and six-port injection valve
- LX-CC column compartment with a column oven and a two-position column switching valve
- LX-UV UV/VIS detector with a deuterium lamp, a tungsten lamp and a 10 mm flow cell

Each module has its own leak sensor in a drip tray at the front of the module.

6.2 Specifications

Flow rate range:               0.05 to 5.00 mL/min
Maximum operating pressure:    60 MPa (600 bar)
Gradient:                      Binary, high-pressure mixing
Column oven range:             5 °C to 80 °C
Sample tray cooling range:     4 °C to 40 °C
Injection volume range:        0.1 to 100 µL
Wavelength range:              190 to 600 nm
Flow cell volume:              10 µL, path length 10 mm

6.3 Operating environment

The system must be operated at a room temperature of 18 °C to 28 °C and a relative humidity below 80 %.
It must stand on a stable bench away from direct sunlight, air-conditioning outlets and vibration.

8. DAILY MAINTENANCE
====================

Daily maintenance is done by the operator on each day the system is used.

8.1 Before the first run of the day

1. Check the instrument logbook for open issues and notes from the previous user.
2. Check that each solvent bottle contains enough mobile phase for the planned sequence plus at least 200 mL.
3. Check that aqueous mobile phases containing buffer were prepared no more than 48 hours ago.
4. Check that the waste container is less than 80 % full; empty it if necessary.
5. Check that the seal wash reservoir and the needle wash reservoir are at least half full.
6. Switch on the degasser and wait until the degasser vacuum is ready.
7. Purge each channel in use at 5 mL/min for 2 minutes.
8. Switch on the detector lamp and let it warm up for at least 30 minutes before analysis.
9. Start the method flow and wait until the baseline is stable for at least 10 minutes.
10. Record the start-up pressure in the logbook and compare it with the reference pressure of the method.
11. If the start-up pressure differs from the reference pressure by more than 10 %, follow section 13.1 or 13.2.
12. Look for leaks at all visible fittings and in all drip trays.

8.2 At the end of the day

1. Flush buffered mobile phases out of the system with purified water and organic solvent (90:10) for 20 minutes.
2. Then flush the system and the column with the storage solvent for the column for at least 10 minutes.
3. Store reversed-phase columns in at least 50 % organic solvent, with end caps fitted.
4. If the system will not be used for the next 24 hours, switch off the detector lamp.
5. Set the flow to 0 mL/min, or to a standby flow of 0.1 mL/min if the next run is the following morning.
6. Record the end of use and any problems in the logbook.


9. WEEKLY MAINTENANCE
=====================

Weekly maintenance is done by the operator on the first working day of each week.

9.1 Seal wash

1. Empty the seal wash reservoir and rinse it with purified water.
2. Fill it with freshly prepared seal wash solution (10 % isopropanol in purified water).
3. Prime the seal wash pump from the instrument menu and check that drops leave the seal wash outlet.

9.2 Needle wash

1. Replace the needle wash solvent with fresh solvent of the composition given in the method.
2. Wipe the outside of the needle wash port with a lint-free tissue moistened with isopropanol.
3. Run the needle wash routine three times and check that the wash port drains freely.

9.3 Drip trays and leak sensors

1. Inspect the drip tray of each module for liquid or salt deposits.
2. Clean the drip trays with purified water and dry them with lint-free tissues.
3. Test each leak sensor by placing one drop of purified water on it; the matching leak error must appear.
4. Dry the sensor, clear the error and record the test in the logbook.

9.4 Solvent bottles and inlet filters

1. Replace any mobile phase older than its expiry date.
2. Inspect the solvent inlet filters; if a filter is discoloured, replace it as in section 10.1.
3. Check that bottle caps are closed and that the solvent lines are not kinked.

9.5 System suitability

1. Run the weekly system suitability test (SST) with the reference standard given in SOP-QC-0120.
2. Record the SST result in the logbook.
3. If the SST fails, do not use the system for analysis and inform the QC Instrument Specialist.


10. MONTHLY MAINTENANCE
=======================

Monthly maintenance is done by the QC Instrument Specialist in the first week of each calendar month.

10.1 Solvent inlet filters

1. Replace the solvent inlet filter (LX-1001) of every channel, whether or not it is discoloured.
2. Purge each channel at 5 mL/min for 2 minutes after the replacement.

10.2 Purge valve frit

1. Open the purge valve and remove the purge valve cap.
2. Replace the PTFE purge valve frit (LX-1004), using clean tweezers.
3. Refit the cap and close the purge valve; purge both channels.

10.3 Inline filter

1. Stop the flow and let the pressure fall below 1 MPa.
2. Replace the inline filter cartridge (LX-1007) between the pump and the autosampler.
3. Restart the flow and check the fittings for leaks.

10.4 Deuterium lamp

1. Read the deuterium lamp operating hours from the detector menu and record them in the logbook.
2. Run the detector intensity test from the detector menu and record the result.
3. The deuterium lamp must be replaced after 2,000 operating hours, or earlier if the intensity test fails.
4. To replace the lamp, switch off the detector and let the lamp cool for at least 15 minutes.
5. Unplug the lamp connector, unscrew the two lamp screws and remove the old lamp by its holder.
6. Insert the new lamp (LX-5001) without touching the quartz window, tighten the screws and plug in the connector.
7. Switch on the detector, reset the lamp hour counter and let the lamp warm up for 30 minutes.
8. Run the wavelength accuracy check described in section 12.3 before using the system.

10.5 Flow cell

1. Flush the flow cell at 1.0 mL/min with purified water for 10 minutes, then with isopropanol for 10 minutes.
2. Run the detector intensity test again and compare it with the result before flushing.

10.6 Static leak test

1. Run the static leak test from the pump menu, which closes the outlet and holds the pump at 40 MPa.
2. The pressure drop over 5 minutes must not exceed 1 MPa.
3. If the test fails, check the fittings and repeat it; if it fails again, inform the service provider.


11. ANNUAL MAINTENANCE
======================

11.1 Preventive maintenance

1. Preventive maintenance (PM) is done once a year by an engineer of the service provider.
2. The PM includes replacement of the piston seals, the outlet check valves, the autosampler needle,
   the needle seat and the injection valve rotor seal.
3. The QC Instrument Specialist is present during the PM and checks the service report before signing it.
4. Piston seals are replaced earlier than the annual PM if a leak appears at a pump head.

11.2 Operational qualification

1. An operational qualification (OQ) is performed once a year, after the preventive maintenance.
2. The OQ is done according to the protocol approved by QA and includes all tests of section 12.
3. The system may be used again only after the OQ report has been approved by QA.


12. CALIBRATION
===============

12.1 Calibration interval

The LX-500 system must be calibrated every 6 months.
It must also be calibrated after any repair that can affect a calibrated parameter, such as a pump repair.
Calibration is done by the QC Instrument Specialist using calibrated reference equipment.
The calibration due date is shown on the green calibration status label on the front of the pump module.

12.2 Calibration parameters

Parameter                   Test conditions                                   Acceptance criterion
Flow rate accuracy          1.0 mL/min, purified water, weighed for 10 min    ±2 % of the set flow
Flow rate precision         1.0 mL/min, 6 measurements                        RSD ≤ 0.5 %
Gradient composition        Step gradient with acetone tracer in channel B    ±1 % absolute
Column oven temperature     40 °C, calibrated temperature probe               ±1 °C of the set point
Wavelength accuracy         Built-in holmium oxide filter, 361 nm band        ±1 nm
Detector noise              Water, 254 nm, 1.0 mL/min, over 10 minutes       ≤ 0.02 mAU
Injection precision         10 µL, 6 injections of caffeine standard          RSD ≤ 1.0 % of peak area
Carry-over                  Blank injection after the highest standard        ≤ 0.1 % of the standard

12.3 Wavelength accuracy check

1. Let the deuterium lamp warm up for at least 1 hour.
2. Start the holmium oxide wavelength check from the detector menu.
3. The detector compares the measured band with the expected wavelength and shows the difference.
4. Record the difference in the calibration record.

12.4 Out-of-tolerance results

1. If any parameter is outside its acceptance criterion, stop using the system at once.
2. Attach a red OUT OF SERVICE label to the pump module.
3. Inform QA within 1 working day of the out-of-tolerance result.
4. The QC Instrument Specialist prepares an impact assessment of all results produced since the last
   passed calibration.
5. After repair, the full calibration of section 12.2 is repeated.
6. The system may be returned to service only after QA has approved the impact assessment and the repeated calibration.

12.5 Calibration records

Each calibration is recorded on form F-QC-0142-02 and filed in the calibration folder of the system.
The green calibration status label is updated with the date of calibration and the next due date.


13. TROUBLESHOOTING
===================

This section lists common symptoms, their likely causes and the actions an operator may take.
If the system shows an error code, see section 14 first.
If the problem remains after the actions below, stop using the system and inform the QC Instrument Specialist.

13.1 High or rising system pressure

Likely causes:
- A blocked inline filter, column frit or capillary.
- Buffer precipitated in the system after switching to a high proportion of organic solvent.
- A column used at a flow rate or temperature other than in the method.
Actions:
1. Remove the column and replace it with a union; if the pressure falls to normal, the column is the cause.
2. If the pressure stays high without the column, loosen fittings one by one from the detector back to the pump.
3. Replace the blocked part, usually the inline filter cartridge.
4. Flush precipitated buffer out with warm purified water at a low flow rate.

13.2 Low or falling system pressure

Likely causes:
- A leak at a fitting, the injection valve or a pump seal.
- Air in a pump head.
- An empty solvent bottle.
Actions:
1. Check all fittings and drip trays for liquid.
2. Check the solvent levels and the solvent lines.
3. Purge each channel in use at 5 mL/min for 2 minutes.

13.3 Pressure fluctuations

Likely causes:
- Air bubbles in a pump head.
- A dirty or worn check valve.
- The degasser not working.
Actions:
1. Check the degasser status.
2. Purge the affected channel.
3. If the fluctuation is limited to one channel, the check valve of that pump head is the likely cause.

13.4 Baseline noise

Likely causes:
- An ageing deuterium lamp.
- Air in the flow cell.
- Poor mixing of the mobile phase.
Actions:
1. Check the lamp operating hours and run the detector intensity test.
2. Flush the flow cell to remove air.
3. Use freshly degassed mobile phase.

13.5 Baseline drift

Likely causes:
- The column or the detector is not fully equilibrated.
- Changes in the room temperature.
- Contaminated mobile phase.
Actions:
1. Equilibrate for longer before starting the sequence.
2. Keep the column compartment door closed during the run.
3. Prepare fresh mobile phase.

13.6 Ghost peaks

Likely causes:
- Contaminated mobile phase or needle wash solvent.
- Carry-over from a previous injection.
Actions:
1. Run a blank gradient without injection; if the peaks remain, the mobile phase is the cause.
2. Replace the needle wash solvent and clean the needle wash port.
3. Inject a blank and check for carry-over as described in section 13.10.

13.7 Retention time drift

Likely causes:
- A change in the mobile phase composition, for example by evaporation of the organic part.
- An unstable column temperature.
- A leak that reduces the actual flow.
Actions:
1. Prepare fresh mobile phase and keep the bottles closed.
2. Check the column oven set point and the column compartment temperature.
3. Check the fittings for leaks.

13.8 Peak tailing or split peaks

Likely causes:
- A void or blocked frit at the column inlet.
- A poorly made fitting with dead volume.
- A sample solvent that is much stronger than the mobile phase.
Actions:
1. Remake the fittings at the column inlet and outlet.
2. Test with a new column of the same type.
3. Dilute the sample in the mobile phase, if the method allows it.

13.9 Poor injection precision

Likely causes:
- Air in the sample loop or the metering device.
- A partly blocked needle or needle seat.
- Too little sample in the vial.
Actions:
1. Flush the needle and the sample loop from the instrument menu.
2. Check the sample volume in the vials and the needle height setting.
3. If the problem remains, the needle seat may need replacement.

13.10 Carry-over

Likely causes:
- A contaminated needle or needle seat.
- An unsuitable needle wash solvent.
Actions:
1. Inject a blank after the highest standard and measure the peak area.
2. If the carry-over is above the criterion in section 12.2, clean the needle seat and change the wash solvent.

13.11 No peaks

Likely causes:
- No sample was injected, for example because of an empty vial or a wrong tray position.
- The detector lamp is off.
- The flow is not reaching the detector.
Actions:
1. Check the vial position and the sample volume.
2. Check that the lamp is on and that the detector signal is being recorded.
3. Check that solvent leaves the detector outlet.


14. ERROR CODES
===============

The LX-500 shows error codes on the module display and in the CDS event log.
Record every error code in the logbook, with the date, time, action taken and result.
Clear an error only after the cause has been removed.
If an error cannot be cleared with the action below, stop using the system and inform the QC Instrument Specialist.

E01  Pump A motor stalled
     Cause:  The drive of pump A cannot turn, usually because a piston is blocked by crystallised buffer.
     Action: Stop the flow, flush pump head A with warm purified water and restart the pump.
             If the error returns, call the service provider.

E02  Pump B motor stalled
     Cause:  The drive of pump B cannot turn, usually because a piston is blocked by crystallised buffer.
     Action: Stop the flow, flush pump head B with warm purified water and restart the pump.
             If the error returns, call the service provider.

E03  Pump A home position not found
     Cause:  The position sensor of pump A did not detect the start position of the piston during initialisation.
     Action: Switch the pump module off for 30 seconds and on again.
             If the error returns, call the service provider.

E04  Pump B home position not found
     Cause:  The position sensor of pump B did not detect the start position of the piston during initialisation.
     Action: Switch the pump module off for 30 seconds and on again.
             If the error returns, call the service provider.

E05  Upper pressure limit exceeded
     Cause:  The pressure rose above the upper pressure limit set in the method, usually because of a blockage.
     Action: Find the blockage by loosening fittings one by one from the detector back to the pump.
             Replace the blocked inline filter, frit or capillary, as in section 13.1.

E06  Lower pressure limit not reached
     Cause:  The pressure fell below the lower pressure limit set in the method, usually because of a leak or air.
     Action: Check all fittings for leaks, check the solvent levels and purge both channels.

E07  Pressure sensor offset out of range
     Cause:  With no flow, the pressure sensor reads more than 0.5 MPa.
     Action: Open the purge valve, wait 1 minute and run the pressure zero routine from the pump menu.
             If the offset remains, call the service provider.

E08  Pressure sensor not responding
     Cause:  The pump controller receives no signal from the pressure sensor, usually because of a loose cable.
     Action: Switch off the pump module, reseat the pressure sensor cable and switch the module on again.
             If the error returns, call the service provider.

E09  Purge valve position error
     Cause:  The purge valve did not reach the open or closed position within the expected time.
     Action: Check that nothing blocks the purge valve knob, then run the valve initialisation from the pump menu.

E10  Seal wash flow not detected
     Cause:  No seal wash flow is detected, usually because the seal wash reservoir is empty or its tubing is kinked.
     Action: Refill the seal wash reservoir with seal wash solution, check its tubing and prime the seal wash pump.

E11  Pressure ripple too high, pump A
     Cause:  The pressure ripple of channel A is above 2 %, usually from air or a dirty check valve in pump head A.
     Action: Purge channel A at 5 mL/min for 3 minutes.
             If the ripple stays high, clean or replace the outlet check valve of pump A.

E12  Pressure ripple too high, pump B
     Cause:  The pressure ripple of channel B is above 2 %, usually from air or a dirty check valve in pump head B.
     Action: Purge channel B at 5 mL/min for 3 minutes.
             If the ripple stays high, clean or replace the outlet check valve of pump B.

E13  Degasser vacuum too low
     Cause:  The degasser cannot reach its working vacuum, usually because of a leak at a degasser connection.
     Action: Tighten the degasser inlet and outlet fittings and switch the degasser off and on.
             If the vacuum stays low, call the service provider.

E14  Degasser vacuum sensor failure
     Cause:  The vacuum sensor of the degasser gives no valid reading.
     Action: Switch the degasser off and on. If the error returns, call the service provider.
             Until the repair, the system may be used only with mobile phases degassed off-line.

E15  Solvent bottle A empty
     Cause:  The level counter of solvent bottle A has fallen below the minimum volume set in the method.
     Action: Refill or replace solvent bottle A, reset the level counter and purge channel A.

E16  Solvent bottle B empty
     Cause:  The level counter of solvent bottle B has fallen below the minimum volume set in the method.
     Action: Refill or replace solvent bottle B, reset the level counter and purge channel B.

E17  Needle position error, vertical
     Cause:  The autosampler needle did not reach the expected height, usually because a vial septum is too hard.
     Action: Check the type of vial caps used, remove any blocked vial and run the needle initialisation.

E18  Needle position error, horizontal
     Cause:  The needle arm did not reach the expected tray position, usually because the sample tray is not seated.
     Action: Remove the sample tray and insert it again until it clicks into place, then run the needle initialisation.

E19  Vial not found
     Cause:  No vial was detected in the tray position given in the sequence.
     Action: Check the sequence and the tray positions, place the vial correctly and restart from that injection.

E20  Injection valve switching timeout
     Cause:  The injection valve did not switch between the load and inject positions within the expected time.
     Action: Run the valve initialisation from the autosampler menu.
             If the error returns, the rotor seal may be worn; call the service provider to replace it.

E21  Sample tray temperature out of range
     Cause:  The sample tray temperature has differed from the set point by more than 3 °C for more than 10 minutes.
     Action: Check that the autosampler door is closed and the cooling vents are free.
             Allow 30 minutes for the tray temperature to stabilise.

E22  Metering device drive error
     Cause:  The autosampler metering piston did not move as expected, usually because the sample loop is blocked.
     Action: Flush the needle and the sample loop with purified water from the autosampler menu.
             If the error returns, call the service provider.

E23  Needle wash flow not detected
     Cause:  No needle wash flow is detected, usually because the needle wash reservoir is empty or its drain is blocked.
     Action: Refill the needle wash reservoir with fresh wash solvent, purge the wash port for 30 seconds
             and check that the wash port drain tubing is not blocked.

E24  Needle seat blocked
     Cause:  The pressure rises sharply at injection, usually because particles have blocked the needle seat.
     Action: Back-flush the needle seat with purified water.
             If the blockage remains, call the service provider to replace the needle seat.

E25  Column compartment temperature above set point
     Cause:  The column compartment is more than 2 °C above the set point, usually because its vents are blocked.
     Action: Check the room temperature and the vents at the rear of the column compartment.
             Allow 20 minutes for the temperature to stabilise.

E26  Column compartment temperature below set point
     Cause:  The column compartment is more than 2 °C below the set point, usually because its door was left open.
     Action: Close the column compartment door and allow 20 minutes for the temperature to stabilise.
             If the error returns, call the service provider.

E27  Column compartment temperature sensor failure
     Cause:  The temperature sensor of the column compartment gives no valid reading.
     Action: Do not use the column compartment until it is repaired, and call the service provider.

E28  Column switching valve error
     Cause:  The column switching valve did not reach the requested position.
     Action: Run the valve initialisation and check that a column is installed in the selected position.
             If the error returns, call the service provider.

E29  Deuterium lamp ignition failed
     Cause:  The deuterium lamp did not ignite, usually because it is at the end of its life or its connector is loose.
     Action: Switch off the detector, let the lamp cool for 15 minutes, check the lamp connector and switch on again.
             If the lamp still does not ignite, replace the deuterium lamp.

E30  Deuterium lamp intensity low
     Cause:  The deuterium lamp energy is below the minimum, usually because of an ageing lamp or a dirty flow cell.
     Action: Flush the flow cell as in section 10.5.
             If the intensity stays low, replace the deuterium lamp.

E31  Lamp operating hours limit reached
     Cause:  The hour counter of the deuterium lamp has reached the replacement limit stored in the detector.
     Action: Replace the deuterium lamp as described in section 10.4 and reset the lamp hour counter.

E32  Tungsten lamp failure
     Cause:  The tungsten lamp for the visible range did not ignite or has burnt out.
     Action: Replace the tungsten lamp (LX-5003).
             Until then, measurements below 400 nm using the deuterium lamp alone may continue.

E33  Reference signal too low
     Cause:  The signal of the reference photodiode is too low, usually because of air in the flow cell.
     Action: Flush the flow cell at 1.0 mL/min for 10 minutes with degassed mobile phase to remove the air.

E34  Wavelength calibration failed
     Cause:  The holmium oxide check at power-on found a wavelength error of more than 1 nm.
     Action: Repeat the check after the lamp has warmed up for 1 hour.
             If it fails again, take the detector out of service and follow section 12.4.

E35  Leak detected, pump
     Cause:  The leak sensor in the pump drip tray detected liquid, usually from a loose fitting or a worn piston seal.
     Action: Stop the flow, find and fix the leak, dry the pump leak sensor with a lint-free tissue and clear the error.

E36  Leak detected, autosampler
     Cause:  The leak sensor in the autosampler drip tray detected liquid, usually from the injection valve.
     Action: Stop the sequence, find and fix the leak, dry the autosampler leak sensor and clear the error.

E37  Leak detected, column compartment
     Cause:  The leak sensor in the column compartment detected liquid, usually from a column fitting that is not tight.
     Action: Stop the flow, tighten or replace the column fittings, dry the column compartment leak sensor
             and clear the error.

E38  Communication timeout
     Cause:  A module did not answer the CDS within 30 seconds, usually because of a loose network cable.
     Action: Check the network cable of the module and restart the instrument control software.
             Do not restart the CDS workstation while a sequence is running on another system.

E39  Firmware mismatch
     Cause:  The firmware versions of the modules are not compatible with each other.
     Action: Do not use the system.
             Firmware is updated only by the service provider, under change control.

E40  Internal memory error
     Cause:  The module failed its memory self-test at power-on.
     Action: Switch the module off for 1 minute and on again.
             If the error returns, take the system out of service and call the service provider.


15. DOCUMENTATION AND RECORDS
=============================

15.1 Every system has its own instrument logbook, identified by the system number on the front of the pump module.
15.2 All maintenance tasks, error codes, repairs and calibrations are recorded in the logbook on the day they happen.
15.3 Each entry contains the date, the task or event, the result and the initials of the person who made it.
15.4 Corrections are made by a single line through the old entry, with the date, initials and reason.
15.5 Service reports from the service provider are filed in the system folder together with the logbook.
15.6 The QC Instrument Specialist reviews each logbook once a month and signs the review.
15.7 Completed logbooks are archived in the QC document archive for 15 years.


16. TRAINING
============

16.1 Operators must be trained on this SOP before they use the LX-500 system without supervision.
16.2 Training consists of reading this SOP and a practical session with the QC Instrument Specialist.
16.3 The QC Instrument Specialist must also be trained by the service provider on the monthly maintenance tasks.
16.4 Training is recorded in the training management system.
16.5 After a new version of this SOP becomes effective, all users must read it and confirm the training again.
